Welcome

The Jewel of
Micronesia

Nestled in the heart of the Pacific Ocean, Nauru offers a unique blend of natural beauty and rich cultural heritage spanning over 3,000 years.

Smallest Republic in the World

Nauru is the world's third-smallest country by area and the second-least populous, after Vatican City. Its phosphate deposits made it one of the wealthiest nations per capita in the 1970s.

Today, Nauru is reclaiming its landscape and writing a new chapter — one of sustainability, cultural revival, and open-hearted welcome to the world.

Discover

Experience the
Island Life

From stunning coral reefs to unique birdlife, from WWII relics to vibrant local culture.

🌊

Surrounded by the Pacific

Nauru sits alone in the western Pacific, a raised coral island ringed by a pristine reef. The ocean defines everything here — from our food to our way of life.

The island's phosphate-rich interior rises to 65 meters at Command Ridge, offering breathtaking 360° views of the endless blue Pacific horizon.

🏖️

Pristine Beaches

Anibare Bay offers crystal-clear waters and white sandy beaches — perfect for snorkeling, swimming, and sunset walks along the Pacific shoreline.

🤝

Community First

Nauruan culture is built on community bonds and the traditional concept of engam — mutual support and sharing across extended family networks.

🤿

Coral Reefs

Explore underwater caves, vibrant coral gardens, and tropical fish in Nauru's protected lagoon. The surrounding reef teems with diverse marine life.

🌿

Natural Rebirth

After decades of phosphate mining, Nauru is reclaiming its landscape through innovative rehabilitation and eco-tourism initiatives.

History

How we got here.

From ancient Micronesian settlement to modern independent republic.

~1000 BCE
First Settlers
Micronesian navigators arrive by outrigger canoe from the Caroline Islands.
1798
"Pleasant Island"
British Captain John Fearn spots Nauru, naming it for its welcoming shores.
1900
Phosphate Rush
Discovery of the world's richest phosphate deposits changes everything.
1968
Independence!
Nauru becomes the world's smallest independent republic on January 31.
Culture

Vibe of the island.

Traditional dance, island flavors, and celebrations that define Nauruan life.

🕺

Dance & Music

Traditional Nauruan dance tells stories of the ocean, the harvest, and community life. Rhythmic hip movements and hand gestures passed down through generations.

🍛

Island Flavors

Coconut, fresh fish, breadfruit, and pandanus — Nauruan cuisine is pure Pacific. Don't leave without trying coconut-crusted reef fish.

🎉

Celebrations

Angam Day (Oct 26) celebrates the Nauruan population reaching 1,500. Constitution Day (May 17) is a week-long festival of culture and community.
