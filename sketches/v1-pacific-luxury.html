"God's Will First"

— National Motto of the Republic of Nauru

Nauru is the world's third-smallest country by area and the second-least populous, after Vatican City. Its phosphate deposits made it one of the wealthiest nations per capita in the 1970s.

Discover

Experience the
Island Life

From stunning coral reefs to unique birdlife, from WWII relics to vibrant local culture.

🏖️

Pristine Beaches

Anibare Bay offers crystal-clear waters and white sandy beaches — perfect for snorkeling, swimming, and sunset walks along the Pacific shoreline.

🤿

Coral Reefs

The surrounding reef teems with marine life. Explore underwater caves, vibrant coral gardens, and tropical fish in Nauru's protected lagoon.

🏛️

Cultural Heritage

Experience traditional Nauruan dance, music, and crafts. The annual Angam Day and Constitution Day celebrations showcase living culture.

🌴

Natural Wonders

Explore the raised coral island's unique ecosystem — from the central plateau to the lush coastal vegetation and incredible bird migration routes.

🌊

Command Ridge

The highest point at 65 meters offers panoramic views of the entire island. A winding road leads to WWII gun emplacements with stunning ocean vistas.

🐟

Pacific Cuisine

Fresh seafood, coconut-based dishes, and tropical fruits define Nauruan cuisine. Don't miss the traditional coconut fish and breadfruit specialties.

History

3,000 Years of
Pacific Heritage

From ancient Micronesian settlers to phosphate discovery and modern independence.

~1000 BCE

First Micronesian settlers arrive by outrigger canoe from the Caroline Islands.

1798

British whaling captain John Fearn discovers Nauru, naming it "Pleasant Island."

1888

Nauru becomes part of the German Empire's Marshall Islands protectorate.

1900

Phosphate discovered — the richest deposits in the world, transforming Nauru's future.

1968

Nauru gains full independence on January 31, with Hammer DeRoburt as first President.

1999

Nauru joins the United Nations as the world's smallest independent republic.
