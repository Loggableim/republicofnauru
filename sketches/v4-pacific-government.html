Discover Nauru

Experience the
Island Life

From stunning coral reefs to unique birdlife, from WWII relics to vibrant local culture.

🏖️

Anibare Bay

Crystal-clear waters and white sandy beaches — perfect for snorkeling, swimming, and sunset walks along the Pacific shoreline. The bay's coral reef protects a pristine lagoon teeming with marine life.

The underwater world features vibrant coral gardens, tropical fish, and occasional sea turtles. Guided snorkeling tours available year-round.

🏛️

Cultural Heritage

Traditional Nauruan dance, music, and crafts. The annual Angam Day and Constitution Day celebrations showcase living culture spanning 3,000 years.

🌊

Command Ridge

The highest point at 65 meters offers panoramic 360° views of the entire island and the endless Pacific horizon.

🌟

Nauru at a Glance

The 12-pointed star on the flag represents Nauru's twelve original tribes. The yellow stripe marks the equator, the blue field is the Pacific Ocean. "God's Will First" — our national motto since independence.

History

3,000 Years of
Pacific Heritage

From ancient Micronesian settlers to phosphate discovery and modern independence.

~1000 BCE
First Settlers
Micronesian navigators arrive by outrigger canoe from the Caroline Islands.
1798
"Pleasant Island"
British Captain John Fearn spots Nauru, naming it for its welcoming shores.
1888
German Protectorate
Nauru becomes part of the German Empire's Marshall Islands protectorate.
1900
Phosphate Rush
Discovery of the world's richest phosphate deposits changes everything forever.
1968
Independence!
Nauru becomes the world's smallest independent republic on January 31, 1968.
1999
UN Member State
Nauru joins the United Nations as the world's smallest independent republic.
Culture & Life

Vibe of the Island

Nauruan culture is built on community bonds, extended family networks, and traditions passed down through generations.

🕺

Dance & Music

Traditional Nauruan dance tells stories of the ocean, the harvest, and community life. Rhythmic hip movements and hand gestures passed down through generations.

🍛

Island Flavors

Coconut, fresh fish, breadfruit, and pandanus — Nauruan cuisine is pure Pacific. Don't leave without trying coconut-crusted reef fish and traditional feasts.

🎉

Celebrations

Angam Day (Oct 26) celebrates the Nauruan population reaching 1,500. Constitution Day (May 17) is a week-long festival of culture, music, and community.
